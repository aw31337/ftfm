# Evidence Travel Risks — Field Discipline Checklist

*Section 2 — PRESERVATION | FTFM v2*

When handling sensitive evidence during incident response, small mistakes create chain-of-custody failures,
legal challenges, or loss of credibility. The following are the most common IR evidence travel risks.

---

## The Seven Critical Rules

**1. Never leave Chain-of-Custody items unattended**
Any item you have signed for must remain under direct visual supervision at all times.
A lapse — even brief — creates a credibility gap that opposing counsel will exploit.

**2. Hand-carry sensitive evidence**
Do not rely on third parties, couriers, or shipping services for sensitive media.
Hand-carry from seizure to lab. If you must ship, document every transfer point.

**3. Lock in a safe when not under direct observation**
If you cannot visually monitor the evidence, it must be secured in a locked container.
Hotel room safes are acceptable when a proper evidence locker is unavailable.

**4. Never check evidence as baggage**
Checked luggage is accessible to airport personnel, can be lost, and has no chain-of-custody protection.
All evidence travels as carry-on, regardless of size or inconvenience.

**5. Never leave evidence in a vehicle — even briefly**
Vehicles are high-risk: break-ins happen in minutes. No exceptions for gas stops, food, or "just a moment."
If you cannot take the evidence with you, stay with the vehicle.

**6. Never expose evidence in open or public areas**
Keep evidence in opaque cases or bags. Do not set it on counters, restaurant tables, or open desks.
Opportunistic theft requires only seconds.

**7. Maintain continuous situational awareness**
Continuously assess your environment when transporting sensitive materials.
Public transport, hotel lobbies, and airports are high-risk environments.
